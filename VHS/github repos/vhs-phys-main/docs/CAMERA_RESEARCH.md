# Camera tube physics: research

Research behind the camera stage (`docs/CAMERA.md`): the physics of 1965-1990 pickup tubes, their data, and what it implies for the model.

## Summary

The look of 1965-1990 tube-camera video comes mostly from one mechanism: a low-velocity electron beam recharging a capacitive photoconductor, one line at a time. Its readout has a well-defined closed form. Philips derived it in 1963. The beam lands current `i = I0·exp(V/VT)`, so the target recovers along a logistic curve whose time constant is **τ = C·VT / i_final**. That one equation explains why lag is worse in low light, why build-up is fast and decay slow, why bias light helps, and why lag is set by elapsed time ([Philips Tech. Rev. 1963](https://worldradiohistory.com/Archive-Company-Publications/Philips-Technical-Review/60s/Philips-Technical-Review-1963.pdf)). One charge constant, K = C·VT of about 0.22-0.37 nC, reproduces the Plumbicon handbook decay curves at every bias level to within about one percentage point (derived here). The same constant predicts the high-key lag to within about 0.3 points. Material traps add a smaller, exposure-dependent tail: in PbO it shows mainly in blue and extended-red layers. In Sb2S3 vidicons, traps dominate lag. The beam can recharge only about **twice the peak-white charge** per scan, so specular highlights become comet tails and blooms unless an anti-comet-tail gun clamps them, which works up to **32× peak white** ([Mullard 1987](https://tubedata.altanatubes.com.br/other/Mullard/Mullard_ElectronicTubes_Book2Part2A_PlumbiconCameraTubes&Accessories_1987.pdf)). For the existing vhs-phys camera stage this means several things. The fitted VT of 0.05-0.07 V is a charge-scale compensation. Real beam temperatures are higher: measured guns run at 1200-2350 K, which is 0.10-0.20 V. The "2-3.5 % biased tail" the model misses is very likely a column of the handbook table that shows unbiased values. Build-up is too slow most likely because dark and bias current are missing from the recovery rate. Model updates should run per field, in real time. Which effects shape the look depends on the camera. For consumer single-tube camcorders, the likeliest source for VHS, these lead: lag of 10-30 % at 50 ms, beam-starved highlights that turn green, 0.5 MHz colour and stripe-carrier artefacts. For three-tube Plumbicon cameras they are comet tails on highlights, low-key lag, and gamma and aperture-correction processing. Fine-detail effects such as head-amplifier noise, 30 ns registration errors and the beam-spot response at 5 MHz fall largely outside a VHS-class recording band.

Evidence labels used throughout: **[DS]** a number printed in a manufacturer data sheet, handbook table, patent or paper text; **[FIG]** a value read by eye from a printed graph (about ±0.5 percentage point on lag plots, ±3-5 % on MTF and build-up plots, ±10 nm on spectra); **[DER]** arithmetic or inference made for this report from cited numbers that no source states directly; **[SEC]** a secondary or abstract-only source. Times are at 50 Hz (20 ms fields) unless stated; "3rd field" means 60 ms at 50 Hz but 50 ms at 60 Hz.

## Six photoconductors differ in gamma, dark current and lag mechanism

Every target in this family is a photoconductive layer on a transparent signal plate. The layer is biased at 10-50 V and scanned on its back by a beam that drives each element to cathode potential. Between visits, light discharges the element, and the charge the beam replaces is the video signal. The materials differ in four properties that matter for simulation: the transfer exponent (gamma), dark current and how fast it grows with temperature, layer capacitance, and how lag divides between traps and beam discharge.

**Lead oxide (Plumbicon, Vistacon)** is a reverse-biased PIN structure 10-18 µm thick, linear to the beam limit with **gamma 0.95 ± 0.05**. It reaches **400 µA/lm** at 2856 K with the signal plate at a fixed 45 V. Because sensitivity is saturated at that voltage, exposure is set by iris and ND filters, never by target voltage ([Philips T10](https://frank.pocnet.net/other/Philips/elcoma/Philips_ElectronTubes_T10_1985_MP.pdf)) [DS]. Dark current is at most 1-3 nA. Philips calls its photoconductive lag "practically negligible": what lag the tube shows is discharge lag "set by layer capacitance and beam resistance", visible at low key and reduced by bias light ([Mullard 1987](https://tubedata.altanatubes.com.br/other/Mullard/Mullard_ElectronicTubes_Book2Part2A_PlumbiconCameraTubes&Accessories_1987.pdf)) [DS]. **Antimony trisulphide (vidicon)** is only 2-3 µm thick and non-linear. Its average gamma over 20-200 nA is **0.55 min / 0.74 typ / 0.85 max**, and its sensitivity is set by target voltage, which is adjusted for about 20 nA dark current ([XQ1271](https://lampes-et-tubes.info/ct/XQ1271.pdf); [Philips T12](https://ftp.mirrorservice.org/sites/www.bitsavers.org/components/philips/_dataBooks/1987_T12_Philips_Vidicon_and_Newvicon_Camera_Tubes.pdf)) [DS]. It is the only common target whose lag is dominated by traps at high signal. **Selenium-arsenic-tellurium (Saticon)** is an amorphous layer about 4 µm thick, used at exactly 50 V, with gamma 1 at every wavelength and dark current about 0.3-0.5 nA. Hitachi says its lag "can be considered to be almost capacitive" ([Hitachi H8397A](https://frank.yueksel.org/other/Hitachi/Hitachi_Saticon_H8397A_1977.pdf)) [DS]. **ZnSe/ZnTe-CdTe (Newvicon)** is linear, three to four times as sensitive as a Plumbicon behind an IR filter, and has "much larger target capacitance". Its lag is therefore "significantly higher than that of a Plumbicon" but free of photoconductive lag ([Philips T12](https://ftp.mirrorservice.org/sites/www.bitsavers.org/components/philips/_dataBooks/1987_T12_Philips_Vidicon_and_Newvicon_Camera_Tubes.pdf)) [DS]. **CdSe (Chalnicon)** has gamma 0.90-0.95, about 2670 µA/lm and lag "mainly due to the capacitive lag" ([Toshiba 1973](https://frank.yueksel.org/other/Toshiba/Toshiba_Chalnicon_1973.pdf)) [DS].

Spectral response separates the materials more sharply than sensitivity does. Standard PbO peaks near 485-500 nm at about **282 mA/W** and falls to zero by about 650 nm. Sulphur-doped "extended red" PbO tails to 850-950 nm and needs an IR-reflecting filter ([Philips T10, Fig. 1](https://frank.pocnet.net/other/Philips/elcoma/Philips_ElectronTubes_T10_1985_MP.pdf)) [FIG]. Sb2S3 peaks near 550 nm and is at half its peak near 675 nm. The Newvicon peaks at 730-740 nm at about 575 mA/W, keeps about 220 mA/W at 400 nm and cuts off near 900 nm (1000 nm for the extended-IR type) ([Philips T12, Fig. 1](https://ftp.mirrorservice.org/sites/www.bitsavers.org/components/philips/_dataBooks/1987_T12_Philips_Vidicon_and_Newvicon_Camera_Tubes.pdf)) [FIG]. The Saticon is blue-peaked: 100 % at 430-450 nm, about 40 % at 595 nm, about 3 % at 700 nm ([Hitachi H8397A, Fig. 3](https://frank.yueksel.org/other/Hitachi/Hitachi_Saticon_H8397A_1977.pdf)) [FIG]. The Chalnicon is flat to rising at 0.3-0.47 µA/µW up to about 710 nm, then falls steeply to near zero by 770 nm ([Toshiba 1973, Fig. 6](https://frank.yueksel.org/other/Toshiba/Toshiba_Chalnicon_1973.pdf)) [FIG]. Converting with `QE = S[A/W]·1240/λ[nm]` gives quantum efficiencies of about 0.71 for PbO at 490 nm, 0.97 for the Newvicon peak and 0.83 for the Chalnicon peak [DER]. The spectral knots in those figures can be interpolated directly into a per-channel response, provided the lens IR-cut filter is included: the Newvicon's 3-4 × figure is quoted behind such a filter.

Two capacitances appear in the data and must not be confused. The data-sheet "signal electrode to all" value, 1.5-6 pF, is the output capacitance that loads the head amplifier ([Philips T10](https://frank.pocnet.net/other/Philips/elcoma/Philips_ElectronTubes_T10_1985_MP.pdf)) [DS]. The layer capacitance that stores the image and sets discharge lag is three orders of magnitude larger. Philips gives **1000-1500 pF for a 10 µm PbO target** and an allowed band of about 800-2000 pF ([Philips Tech. Rev. 1963](https://worldradiohistory.com/Archive-Company-Publications/Philips-Technical-Review/60s/Philips-Technical-Review-1963.pdf)) [DS]. Hitachi measured the Saticon layer at about 1000-2000 pF/cm², strongly frequency-dispersive and independent of bias ([Hitachi Hyoron 1976, Fig. 10](https://www.hitachihyoron.com/jp/pdf/1976/04/1976_04_15.pdf)) [FIG]. That is about 580-1160 pF on a 2/3-inch target [DER]. No permittivity or thickness was found for the Newvicon or Chalnicon layers.

## One logistic law explains Plumbicon lag from a single charge constant

### The beam lands current exponentially, so recovery is logistic

Seen from the target, the beam is a nonlinear resistor. Electrons leave the cathode with a quasi-Maxwellian spread of axial energy. While the scanned surface sits below cathode potential, only the faster electrons land, so the landing current is

```
i_land = I0 · exp(V / VT),   VT = k·T_b / e          (exponential region of the acceptance curve)
```

Here V is the surface potential relative to the cathode and T_b is the effective beam temperature ([Philips Tech. Rev. 1963, p. 146](https://worldradiohistory.com/Archive-Company-Publications/Philips-Technical-Review/60s/Philips-Technical-Review-1963.pdf); [US 4,388,556](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4388556)) [DS]. Above cathode potential the whole beam is accepted, which is the saturated shoulder. Kurashige (NHK) adds a second, much hotter exponential region two to three decades below the peak current, which he models as a fraction α of the beam at about 10,000 K. It matters only if the dark equilibrium sits inside it ([Kurashige 1978](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/itej1978/32/3/32_3_217/_pdf)) [DS]. **Measured beam temperatures are 1900-2350 K for triode guns with a crossover, about 1200 K for crossover-free (diode) guns, and 1300-1500 K for a low-noise crossover design** ([US 4,388,556](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4388556)) [DS], so VT = 0.10-0.20 V [DER]. Electron-electron scattering at the crossover (the Boersch effect) heats the beam above the cathode temperature. Kurashige identifies this as the dominant control on decay lag, with convergence angle, landing tilt and contact-potential spread secondary ([Kurashige 1978](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/itej1978/32/3/32_3_217/_pdf)) [DS].

With a uniformly lit target and a continuously supplying beam, the element obeys `C·dV/dt = i_photo − i_land`. For a step in photocurrent from i1 to i2, Philips solved this exactly ([Philips Tech. Rev. 1963, p. 147, Eq. 6](https://worldradiohistory.com/Archive-Company-Publications/Philips-Technical-Review/60s/Philips-Technical-Review-1963.pdf)) [DS]:

```
i_land(t) = i2 / [ 1 − (1 − i2/i1) · exp(−t · i2 / K) ],      K = C·VT
```

The authors state that the result holds when the charge is delivered in surges by the scan. They list its properties [DS]: lag is independent of beam current in the exponential regime; it falls with capacitance; it is shorter the higher the new light level; and it is shorter the cooler the beam. Near the knee the first property fails, and lag then falls with beam current. Crowell and Madden derived exact rise and decay transients for the same regime. They found the decay "independent of initial signal strength" and confirmed it on silicon-diode vidicons ([Crowell & Madden 1971, abstract](https://scilit.com/publications/9288701185622df52c59c2cbe6ecc41d)) [SEC].

Three consequences follow directly [DER]. First, the relaxation rate is set by the **final** current. Build-up heads toward signal plus dark plus bias, so it is fast. Decay heads toward dark plus bias alone, so it is slow. Bias light shortens decay by raising i2. Second, for decay with i2 ≪ i1 and t ≪ τ the law reduces to **i_land ≈ K/t**. This is a universal hyperbolic start that does not depend on the initial signal, so lag expressed as a percentage falls in proportion to signal level. Third, in a discrete scan the exponential law is linear in `u = exp(−V/VT)`. One beam pass with dwell t gives `u ← u + I0·t/(C·VT)`. A storage interval T with charging current i_s gives `u ← u·exp(−i_s·T/(C·VT))`. So per field `u_{n+1} = r·u_n + κ`, with `r = exp(−(i_d + i_bias)·T_f/(C·VT))` and `κ = I0·T_f/(C·VT)`. The equilibrium `I0·exp(V*/VT) = i_d + i_bias` does not depend on the field period (algebra from Kurashige Eqs. 1 and 3, [Kurashige 1978](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/itej1978/32/3/32_3_217/_pdf)).

### One constant reproduces the handbook curves

The XQ1520/XQ1410 handbook figures give full decay and build-up curves against bias light for each channel. Conditions: 30 mm tube, 2856 K lamp behind OG570/VG9/BG12 filters, 5 s of light before decay, 10 s of dark before build-up, Is/Ib = 20/300 nA for R and B or 40/600 nA for L and G, 50 Hz. Here **Ib means the largest signal the beam can stabilise, which differs from the beam current** ([Mullard 1987](https://tubedata.altanatubes.com.br/other/Mullard/Mullard_ElectronicTubes_Book2Part2A_PlumbiconCameraTubes&Accessories_1987.pdf)) [DS]. Fitting the red-tube decay family with a single **K = 0.225 nC** (signal 20 nA, dark 1 nA, residual measured above dark) gives the following [DER against FIG]:

| XQ1520R decay, % of initial | 60 ms | 80 ms | 120 ms | 200 ms |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 nA bias: curve [FIG] / law [DER] | 13 / 13.5 | 9 / 10.0 | 5.3 / 6.3 | 3.3 / 3.2 |
| 3 nA bias: curve / law | 8.3 / 8.0 | 5.5 / 5.0 | 2.4 / 2.2 | 0.6 / 0.5 |
| 8 nA bias: curve / law | 3.0 / 3.0 | 1.9 / 1.3 | 0.25 / 0.3 | n/a |

For build-up with no bias the curve reads **4 / 52 / 85 / 96 %** at 20 / 40 / 60 / 80 ms [FIG]. The law gives 21 / 66 / 93 / 99 % [DER], early by about half a field, which is consistent with a beam that samples once per field. At 1, 3 and 8 nA bias the law gives 97, 99 and 100 % at 60 ms against 97, 98 and 99.5 % read from the curves ([Mullard 1987, Figs. 7 and 12](https://tubedata.altanatubes.com.br/other/Mullard/Mullard_ElectronicTubes_Book2Part2A_PlumbiconCameraTubes&Accessories_1987.pdf)). Fits to individual rows of the unbiased XQ1520 table give K = 0.22-0.27 nC for R, 0.27-0.37 for L/G, 0.25-0.34 for B and 0.31-0.57 for the extended-red types. K fitted to build-up is 1.1-1.5 times K fitted to decay, so a single constant is good to about 30 % [DER].

The law also predicts how lag depends on signal level, which no fit used. At high key (300 nA signal, beam 600 nA) the XQ1020 sheet prints **1.2 % at 60 ms and 0.4 % at 200 ms** ([Philips 5b 1975](https://frank.pocnet.net/other/Philips/elcoma/Philips_ElectronTubes_5b_1975-05.pdf)) [DS]. The logistic law with K = 0.225-0.30 nC and 1 nA dark gives 1.1-1.5 % and 0.23-0.35 % [DER]. This is the strongest available evidence that one beam-discharge mechanism, parameterised by K = C·VT, carries Plumbicon lag across the whole tonal range.

The constant is physically reasonable. K = 0.225 nC corresponds to C = 1.3 nF at 2000 K or 2.2 nF at 1200 K, inside the Philips 1000-2000 pF range [DER]. Philips' own design rule is a time constant under 1/25 s at 10 nA with C ≤ 2000 pF, which implies VT ≈ 0.2 V (about 2300 K) [DER from DS]. That matches the patent's triode-gun figures. As a cross-check on charge scale, a 2/3-inch Saticon layer of 580-1160 pF holding 200 nA for a 60 Hz field stores 2.9-5.7 V [DER]. A patent says 2/3-inch Plumbicons "typically develop a 5 volt charge image" and 6-7 V p-p at 100 % video ([US 4,540,919](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4540919A/en)) [DS].

### Traps add an exposure-dependent tail, strongest in blue PbO and in Sb2S3

Beam discharge does not explain everything. Under identical beam conditions (20/300 nA) the PbO blue and extended-red tubes have higher decay and slower build-up than L/G. The XQ1520 table, unbiased, gives 9/3 % for L/G, 13/3.5 % for R, 15/5.5 % for B and 15/5 % for ER at 60/200 ms, and build-up at 60 ms of 95, 85, 70 and 65 % ([Mullard 1987](https://tubedata.altanatubes.com.br/other/Mullard/Mullard_ElectronicTubes_Book2Part2A_PlumbiconCameraTubes&Accessories_1987.pdf)) [DS]. Because the beam settings are the same, this dependence on channel must come from the layer [DER]. The cleanest sign is a **bias-independent floor in the blue tube**. The XQ1410B decay at 200 ms reads 5.2 % (0 nA), 3.6 (1 nA), 2.9 (2 nA), 2.6 (3 nA), 2.4 (4 nA), 2.2 (5 nA), 2.1 (8 nA) and 2.0 % (12 nA). All curves converge on about 2 %, while R and G fall to 0.3-1.3 % ([Mullard 1987, Figs. 12 and 14](https://tubedata.altanatubes.com.br/other/Mullard/Mullard_ElectronicTubes_Book2Part2A_PlumbiconCameraTubes&Accessories_1987.pdf)) [FIG]. Philips' 1963 trap theory supports a layer origin. Traps set the speed of approach to steady state, and with a slightly N-type I-layer, hole capture near the N side changes "particularly its sensitivity to blue" ([Philips Tech. Rev. 1963](https://worldradiohistory.com/Archive-Company-Publications/Philips-Technical-Review/60s/Philips-Technical-Review-1963.pdf)) [DS]. RCA likewise says sulphur doping "produces some impurity centers that may trap the charge carriers", giving extended-red Vistacons more image retention ([RCA 1977, AN-5042](https://frank.pocnet.net/other/RCA/RCA_CameraReadyBoroadcastVidiconSeries_1977.pdf)) [DS].

The data that best separate trap lag from beam lag are RCA's decay-versus-exposure families. Conditions: 30 nA signal, 5 nA bias, beam set to 100 nA, 60 Hz, 64 fields of darkness between exposures ([Handbook ch. 11, Figs. 11-61/11-62](http://www.r-type.org/pdfs/pctdh.pdf)). The text states the conclusion [DS]: Saticon lag changes "very little ... as a function of exposure time", while lead-oxide lag is "higher and caused by trapping effects" for longer exposures.

| Exposure (fields at 60 Hz) | PbO XQ1427B, blue light: 50 ms / 200 ms [FIG] | Saticon BC4391: 50 ms / 200 ms [FIG] |
|---|---|---|
| 2 (0.03 s) | 1.9 % / 0.3 % | 1.5 % / 0.2 % |
| 4 | 2.4 / 0.45 | 1.8 / 0.25 |
| 16 | 3.4 / 1.25 | 2.3 / 0.45 |
| 64 (1.07 s) | 4.5 / 1.95 | 2.45 / 0.5 |
| 256 (4.27 s) | 4.7 / 2.25 | 2.55 / 0.5 |

From 2 to 256 fields of exposure, the PbO blue 200 ms tail grows about 7.5× and saturates after roughly one second of exposure. The Saticon tail grows about 2.5× [DER]. A single filling exponential (τ ≈ 0.5 s, ceiling 4.75 %) fits the long exposures but over-predicts the short ones. The 200 ms tail also grows much faster than the 50 ms value. So at least two trap populations, or a capacitive baseline near 1.5 % plus one trap, are needed [DER]. No source gives trap time constants, densities or dispersive-transport fits for any of these materials.

Sb2S3 differs in kind. "At low signal currents discharge lag dominates whereas at high signal currents photoconductive lag is preponderant". The 1-inch XQ1240 leaves **8 % at 200 ms** after a 200 nA signal with 20 nA dark current ([Philips T12](https://ftp.mirrorservice.org/sites/www.bitsavers.org/components/philips/_dataBooks/1987_T12_Philips_Vidicon_and_Newvicon_Camera_Tubes.pdf)) [DS]. In vidicons, the slow response at low illumination "is due entirely to the photoconductive lag" ([Philips Tech. Rev. 1966](https://worldradiohistory.com/Archive-Company-Publications/Philips-Technical-Review/60s/Philips-Technical-Review-1966.pdf)) [DS]. RCA's film vidicon at 300 nA signal and 10 nA dark current reads 13 / 6.3 / 4.5 / 3.3 % at 50 / 100 / 150 / 200 ms. With 20 nA of bias it reads 9.2 / 4.1 / 2.1 / 1.2 %. Raising faceplate illumination from 5 to 150 fc cuts lag 5:1 ([RCA 1977, AN-5012A](https://frank.pocnet.net/other/RCA/RCA_CameraReadyBoroadcastVidiconSeries_1977.pdf)) [FIG/DS]. Consumer-class Sb2S3 tubes at 60 Hz typically leave **20 % after 3 fields (50 ms)** at 200 nA ([Hamamatsu SC-5-2](https://frank.pocnet.net/other/Hamamatsu/Hamamatsu_SC-5-2.pdf); [Matsushita brochure](https://frank.pocnet.net/other/Matsushita/Matsushita_VidiconTubes.pdf)) [DS].

The Saticon is mostly but not wholly capacitive. With 0 / 5 / 10 / about 15-16 nA of equivalent bias light, the low-lag H8397A at 0.2 µA p-p leaves about **6.7 / 3.9 / 2.0 / 0.8 % at 50 ms**, at 30 °C on a 6.6 × 8.8 mm scan ([Hitachi H8397A, Fig. 6](https://frank.yueksel.org/other/Hitachi/Hitachi_Saticon_H8397A_1977.pdf)) [FIG]. The older H8397 leaves about 11 % unbiased. Hitachi attributes the improvement to a gun change that "lowered an equivalent electron temperature", which is exactly the VT lever in the logistic law [DS]. H8397A Fig. 6 also prints a lower unbiased lag at 0.05 µA (2-3 %) than at 0.2 µA (6.7 %), the opposite of the 1/signal scaling the law predicts, but that rise appears only in H8397A Fig. 6. Three independent measurements of the same kind of tube contradict it and fall with signal as a beam-limited capacitor does: RCA's equivalent BC4908 reads **12.4 / 8.7 / 3.6 %** at 30 / 50 / 200 nA unbiased (**6.1 / 4.3 / 2.4 %** at 5 nA bias, **2.9 / 2.2 / 1.85 %** at 10 nA bias; Neuhauser 1978 Fig. 12 in [RCA 1977](https://frank.pocnet.net/other/RCA/RCA_CameraReadyBoroadcastVidiconSeries_1977.pdf)) [FIG], [Hitachi Hyoron 1976](https://www.hitachihyoron.com/jp/pdf/1976/04/1976_04_15.pdf) reports about **3 %** at 200 nA (third field after cut-off) [DS], and [US 4,330,733, Fig. 6](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4330733A/en) reads about **2.4 %** at 0.2 µA [FIG].

### Lag runs on elapsed time

All Philips lag points are stated "at 60 ms and 200 ms (at 50 Hz)" and plotted in 20 ms fields ([Mullard 1987](https://tubedata.altanatubes.com.br/other/Mullard/Mullard_ElectronicTubes_Book2Part2A_PlumbiconCameraTubes&Accessories_1987.pdf)) [DS]. Philips' 1963 test pattern produced pulses "spaced at intervals of 1/50 s (half a frame period because of the interlaced scanning)" ([Philips Tech. Rev. 1963](https://worldradiohistory.com/Archive-Company-Publications/Philips-Technical-Review/60s/Philips-Technical-Review-1963.pdf)) [DS]. Kurashige treats the storage time as the field time because the beam overlaps adjacent lines ([Kurashige 1978](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/itej1978/32/3/32_3_217/_pdf)) [DS]. The physical constant is τ = K/i, so at 60 Hz the curve is the same in time and only the sampling instants move. With K = 0.225 nC the red tube's 3rd-field residual is **16.1 % at 50 ms** (60 Hz) against 13.5 % at 60 ms (50 Hz) [DER]. A model that keeps per-field coefficients fitted at 50 Hz and steps them at 60 Hz over-predicts persistence per unit time. A model that stores one charge per frame halves the visit rate. The first build-up point at one field (4-8 % unbiased) argues against frame-rate visits [DER].

## Beam capacity of twice peak white turns highlights into comet tails

Without special guns, Plumbicon practice sets the beam to **twice the current needed to stabilise normal peak white**. More beam improves highlights, but "very high beam currents ... cause increased lag, some loss of resolution, geometry shifts and reduction of tube life" ([Mullard 1987](https://tubedata.altanatubes.com.br/other/Mullard/Mullard_ElectronicTubes_Book2Part2A_PlumbiconCameraTubes&Accessories_1987.pdf)) [DS]. Peak current on a waveform monitor exceeds the integrated meter current by `100/(100 − blanking %)`, which is 1.3 for CCIR [DS]. Saticon cameras ran the beam at 3-4 × the highlight current ([RCA 1977](https://frank.pocnet.net/other/RCA/RCA_CameraReadyBoroadcastVidiconSeries_1977.pdf)) [DS]. The transfer is linear "up to a point determined by the available beam current"; above it, the curve "turns sharply horizontal" ([Philips Tech. Rev. 1966](https://worldradiohistory.com/Archive-Company-Publications/Philips-Technical-Review/60s/Philips-Technical-Review-1966.pdf)) [DS]. This is the saturated shoulder of the acceptance curve. The existing "clip at the beam current" represents it.

Comet tails and blooming follow from what happens above the shoulder. An unstabilised element charges toward the signal-plate potential. The photocurrent then falls as the field across the layer collapses, and the element "will start to attract electrons towards it when the beam approaches, and continue to do so after the beam should have passed". So "lighter-coloured parts of the scene will be 'blown up'" ([Philips Tech. Rev. 1963, pp. 137-138](https://worldradiohistory.com/Archive-Company-Publications/Philips-Technical-Review/60s/Philips-Technical-Review-1963.pdf)) [DS]. Philips kept the target swing within about **10 V** in a field, because larger swings deflect the approaching beam excessively. This is the stated scale for beam bending [DS]. Re-stabilising takes "a number of scannings", so "'comet tails' can occur behind a moving object" ([Mullard 1987](https://tubedata.altanatubes.com.br/other/Mullard/Mullard_ElectronicTubes_Book2Part2A_PlumbiconCameraTubes&Accessories_1987.pdf)) [DS]. A rough charge balance gives the tail length [DER]. The beam removes about two peak-white swings per field, which is 2.8-4.6 V on a 30 mm red tube with C = 1.3-2.2 nF at 150 nA. The stored overload is bounded by the 45 V signal plate. Clearing it therefore takes up to about 10-16 fields (0.2-0.3 s), after which the residue decays by the logistic law. No measured tail length exists to check this. The only timed observation is that Saticon residues "disappear in approximately 2 s" with the beam at 3-4 × highlight ([RCA 1977, Neuhauser 1978](https://frank.pocnet.net/other/RCA/RCA_CameraReadyBoroadcastVidiconSeries_1977.pdf)) [DS]. Trail colour is material-specific. The Saticon persistent image is white, while lead oxide leaves a "red-trail" ([RCA 1977](https://frank.pocnet.net/other/RCA/RCA_CameraReadyBoroadcastVidiconSeries_1977.pdf)) [DS]. This fits the red channel's lower beam setting and weaker saturation [DER].

Highlight guns change the overload capacity by an order of magnitude. The **ACT gun** (anti-comet-tail) pulses grid 3 during line flyback to defocus the beam onto the limiter aperture. It raises grid 1 to increase beam current to about 100 µA, and lifts the cathode by a few volts. During flyback the beam then recharges any element above the raised cathode level and leaves others untouched. "Blooming and comet-tails are strongly reduced" ([Mullard 1987](https://tubedata.altanatubes.com.br/other/Mullard/Mullard_ElectronicTubes_Book2Part2A_PlumbiconCameraTubes&Accessories_1987.pdf)) [DS]. The XQ1520 cathode-pulse presets of **7 V (B/W, G) and 3.5 V (R, B)** set the "ACT cutting level". The tube handles "**5 lens stops**", a highlight of 10 % picture-height diameter at **32 × peak white**. During flyback the preamplifier sees 15-45 µA [DS]. The 3.5/7 V levels are about 1.5-2.5 × the peak-white charge swing, matching a flyback clamp at about twice peak-white charge [DER]. Marconi adds that the defocused flyback beam covers "many scanning lines". It pre-discharges highlights a few lines ahead, which is why ACT also cuts blooming, but a large overload at the very top of the picture is not pre-discharged ([US 4,143,305](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4143305A/en)) [DS]. The **diode gun with dynamic beam control** raises grid 1 when it detects a sharp rise in signal current and handles **16 × peak white** (4 stops) over the same 10 % spot ([Mullard 1987](https://tubedata.altanatubes.com.br/other/Mullard/Mullard_ElectronicTubes_Book2Part2A_PlumbiconCameraTubes&Accessories_1987.pdf)) [DS]. One Ampex scheme holds the beam at 600 nA up to 300 nA of video, then keeps it 300 nA above the video ([US 4,229,767](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4229767A/en)) [DS]. Japan's return-beam ABO claims "up to 16 times" dynamic-range improvement ([ITE 1978 abstract](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/browse/itej1978/32/0/_contents/-char/en)) [SEC]. With no crossover, the diode gun runs at a cooler beam of about 1200 K. Yet the XQ3440 (diode) and XQ1520 (ACT triode) print identical low-key lag, 9/3 % for L/G, because thinner layers with more capacitance spend the gain ([Mullard 1987](https://tubedata.altanatubes.com.br/other/Mullard/Mullard_ElectronicTubes_Book2Part2A_PlumbiconCameraTubes&Accessories_1987.pdf)) [DS/DER].

Optical flare adds a highlight halo before the beam ever sees the light. Target reflectance is highest in PbO, especially in red, which is why a 6 mm anti-halation disc is fitted. The Saticon flare test gives **1 % (centre) / 0.5 % (corner)** against **5 % / 1 %** for a vidicon, measured as the spurious signal in a black square of 1/10 picture height on a 200 nA white field ([Hitachi H8397A, Table 1](https://frank.yueksel.org/other/Hitachi/Hitachi_Saticon_H8397A_1977.pdf); [Philips T10](https://frank.pocnet.net/other/Philips/elcoma/Philips_ElectronTubes_T10_1985_MP.pdf)) [DS].

## Interlaced readout with an oversized spot blurs, skews and twitters

The beam spot is larger than one frame-line pitch, so every read lands part of its current on the other field's lines. A 1-inch magnetic vidicon measured with a pinhole has a spot of **20-30 µm**, "very similar to the Gaussian shape" ([Sakusabe & Kiuchi 1983](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/itej1978/37/9/37_9_717/_article/-char/en)) [DS]. The diameter convention is not stated. Converting the Philips 400 TVL modulation depths to an equivalent Gaussian (dividing out the 80-85 % test lens and using sine ≈ π/4 of square-wave response) gives sigma of 6-14 µm, FWHM 14-34 µm, across formats [DER]. The 1-inch value, FWHM 25 µm, falls inside the measured 20-30 µm. On 575 active lines this is **sigma ≈ 0.55-0.73 of the frame-line pitch** in every format. Designers scaled the spot with the raster [DER].

Modulation depths from [Philips T10](https://frank.pocnet.net/other/Philips/elcoma/Philips_ElectronTubes_T10_1985_MP.pdf) and [RCA 1977](https://frank.pocnet.net/other/RCA/RCA_CameraReadyBoroadcastVidiconSeries_1977.pdf). Each sigma includes all blur, so it is an upper bound on the beam alone.

In the time domain, the horizontal aperture is the spot moving at scan speed. In the video it appears as a Gaussian of `σ_t = σ_x / v(t)` [DER]. Signal current is itself proportional to scan speed: "signal current is a function of target illumination and of scanning speed" ([Philips T10, 4.2](https://frank.pocnet.net/other/Philips/elcoma/Philips_ElectronTubes_T10_1985_MP.pdf)) [DS]. Sweep non-linearity therefore changes local gain and local sharpness together. Resolution varies by channel. In the same tube type, blue resolves best and red worst: XQ1020 gives 50 / 40 / 35 % for B / G / R at 400 TVL [DS]. This fits deeper red absorption and lateral spreading in the thick PbO layer. In thin layers (Sb2S3 2-3 µm, Saticon about 4 µm) the gun sets resolution, and Saticon response is the same in all colours ([Philips T12](https://ftp.mirrorservice.org/sites/www.bitsavers.org/components/philips/_dataBooks/1987_T12_Philips_Vidicon_and_Newvicon_Camera_Tubes.pdf); [Hitachi H8397A](https://frank.yueksel.org/other/Hitachi/Hitachi_Saticon_H8397A_1977.pdf)) [DS]. For validation there are full curves. The RCA BC4909 Saticon reads about 80 / 62 / 45 / 12 % at 200 / 300 / 400 / 600 TVL at 500 nA signal and 1000 nA beam. The Philips XQ1083 (white) reads about 87 / 67 / 43 / 27 % at 2 / 4 / 6 / 8 MHz ([RCA 1977](https://frank.pocnet.net/other/RCA/RCA_CameraReadyBoroadcastVidiconSeries_1977.pdf); [Philips 5b 1975](https://frank.pocnet.net/other/Philips/elcoma/Philips_ElectronTubes_5b_1975-05.pdf)) [FIG]. Corner limiting resolution is 25-35 % below centre for Sb2S3 and Newvicon (XQ1274: 650 → 450 TVL) and about 20 % below for the Chalnicon. Electrostatic-focus tubes run 10-25 points lower than magnetic ones ([Philips T12](https://ftp.mirrorservice.org/sites/www.bitsavers.org/components/philips/_dataBooks/1987_T12_Philips_Vidicon_and_Newvicon_Camera_Tubes.pdf); [Heimann 1978](https://frank.pocnet.net/other/Heimann/Heimann_ImagePick-upTubes_1978.pdf)) [DS]. More beam blurs: the H8397A centre response falls from about 58 % at low beam to about 37 % at 800 nA ([Hitachi H8397A, Fig. 9](https://frank.yueksel.org/other/Hitachi/Hitachi_Saticon_H8397A_1977.pdf)) [FIG].

Vertically, a Gaussian with sigma/p = 0.55-0.73 puts about 51-64 % of its current on the line being read and **17-23 % on each adjacent line of the other field** [DER]. No source states how this affects integration, so the following is an inference [DER]. At low key the beam has 15-30× more reserve than the signal needs. Even the wing then drives the neighbouring line back to equilibrium, so every element is re-stabilised each field and behaves as if integrating over one field. That fits field-rate lag curves. Near peak white the wing no longer suffices, and integration stretches toward a frame. A small-signal linear estimate gives an effective window of 31-33 ms at 25 Hz. **The integration window is therefore level-dependent.** A per-line Gaussian beam with acceptance-limited landing produces this without any extra parameter. The same spot gives a vertical response of only 0.07-0.25 at frame Nyquist but 0.52-0.71 at field Nyquist [DER]. Fine horizontal detail at field frequency survives and flickers. BBC Monograph 45 calls this "twittering", with horizontal edges vibrating "at picture frequency" and line structure alternating in sign at picture rate ([BBC Monograph 45](https://worldradiohistory.com/UK/BBC/BBC-Technical/BBC-Monograph/bbc-monograph-45-OCR.pdf)) [DS]. Its Kell-factor review gives 0.64-0.7 for sequential scanning and 0.42 for interlaced, while noting that these subjective values are not very reliable [DS].

Because each line is read at its own time, the camera is a long-window rolling shutter. Line k of field F is read at `t_F + k·T_line`, so the top-to-bottom skew is about 18.4 ms of active picture at 625/50 [DER]. Moving verticals shear, and horizontal motion blur spans the full integration window. The scene must be sampled at each line's own read time.

Static errors complete the spatial picture. Philips deflection units allow **0.5 % of picture height of geometric distortion inside a circle of diameter H and 1 % outside**, with skew of 0.4-1 %. Vidicon coil units allow 2 % with 4 % skew. In matched triplets, misregistration is held to **25-40 ns (zone A), 40-80 ns (B) and 80-120 ns (C)**, where 1 ns ≈ 0.00256 % of picture height ([Philips T10](https://frank.pocnet.net/other/Philips/elcoma/Philips_ElectronTubes_T10_1985_MP.pdf); [Philips T12](https://ftp.mirrorservice.org/sites/www.bitsavers.org/components/philips/_dataBooks/1987_T12_Philips_Vidicon_and_Newvicon_Camera_Tubes.pdf)) [DS]. Dynamic registration errors above 0.1 % (zone 1) and up to 0.5 % (zone 3) were brought to 0.05 % and 0.1 % by electronic correction in 1984 ([Iwabe et al. 1984](https://journal.smpte.org/periodicals/SMPTE%20Journal/93/7/17/)) [SEC]. Black shading of the bias-induced dark current may reach **12.5 %** at the corners and has "parabolic and sawtooth components in both line and frame direction". White shading is "a few per cent" centre-high for PbO red and green (blue is flat) and up to 15 % on the RCA Saticon ([Philips T10](https://frank.pocnet.net/other/Philips/elcoma/Philips_ElectronTubes_T10_1985_MP.pdf); [RCA 1977](https://frank.pocnet.net/other/RCA/RCA_CameraReadyBoroadcastVidiconSeries_1977.pdf)) [DS]. Blemishes are specified by a "spot nuisance value", size (% PH) × contrast (%), with fixed counts allowed per zone. Mesh microphonics are named as a defect that rises with grid-3 voltage, with no amplitude data [DS].

## Head-amplifier noise and processing lie mostly above 2.5 MHz

The tube adds essentially no noise. "The Plumbicon tube not generating noise to any noticeable extent, the signal-to-noise ratio will be determined mainly by the input noise of the video amplifier" ([Philips 5b 1975](https://frank.pocnet.net/other/Philips/elcoma/Philips_ElectronTubes_5b_1975-05.pdf)) [DS]. The tube is a current source into 15-30 pF of tube, coil, FET and stray capacitance. The head amplifier equalises that 1/(jωC) roll-off, which multiplies the FET's white voltage noise by f. The result is the "triangular" spectrum, rising in amplitude with frequency, which the BBC observed at the output of Plumbicon head amplifiers ([Jones 1968](https://worldradiohistory.com/Archive-Catalogs/Sellmeyer/Books/Color-Television-Journal-of-the-SMPTE-1970.pdf); [US 4355335](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4355335A/en)) [DS]. A current-referred model is `i_n²(f) = 2q·I_s + 4kT/R_f + (2πfC)²·4kT·R_n`, with an FET equivalent noise resistance R_n = 50-100 Ω [DS] and feedback resistors of about 300 kΩ-1 MΩ [DS]. Over 5 MHz it gives 0.55-1.6 nA rms for C = 15-30 pF [DER]. That brackets the "about 1 nA, as low as 0.5 nA" of good preamplifiers ([US 4704635](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4704635A/en)) [DS]. Philips defines a "visual equivalent" S/N: highlight current divided by rms noise current, times 3, assuming a 2 nA preamplifier in 5 MHz. On that basis a tube was specified at ≥ 200:1 at 0.15 µA ([Philips 5b 1975](https://frank.pocnet.net/other/Philips/elcoma/Philips_ElectronTubes_5b_1975-05.pdf)) [DS]. Hitachi gives > 50 dB at 0.2 µA green without gamma, and Matsushita "approx. 300:1" for the Newvicon ([Hitachi H8397A](https://frank.yueksel.org/other/Hitachi/Hitachi_Saticon_H8397A_1977.pdf); [Matsushita S4075](https://frank.pocnet.net/other/Matsushita/Matsushita_S4075_Vidicon.pdf)) [DS]. Red and blue run 2.5-6 dB worse than green simply because their highlight currents are lower: 150 nA against 200-300 nA [DER].

The f² shape decides how much of this noise reaches tape. In a flat 0-5 MHz band, **87.5 % of the noise power lies above 2.5 MHz and 78 % above 3 MHz** [DER]. A recording channel whose luminance band ends near 3 MHz therefore removes most camera noise. The VHS stage's actual band edge should be substituted. Two places keep it. Near black, gamma multiplies noise by the slope of the linear segment: 4.5 for BT.709 and 5 for the BBC 0.4 law, which is +13 to +14 dB over a unity law ([EBU Tech 3335](https://tech.ebu.ch/files/live/sites/tech/files/shared/tech/tech3335.pdf); [DER]). The other is aperture correction, which lifts the band where the noise lives. A test with a vidicon simulator found that a triangular spectrum "requires a higher detection threshold than white noise with the same signal-to-noise ratio" ([SMPTE J. 89/10, 1980](https://journal.smpte.org/periodicals/SMPTE%20Journal/89/10/6/)) [SEC].

Processing followed a stable order. One 1977 patent describes the channel as: cable compensation; stray-light correction and DC clamp ("black level is set to zero volts"); variable gain; vertical and black shading correction; colour-correction matrix; gamma; clamp; then a blanking and clipping amplifier that "clips at the black and white levels and adds the aperture correction signal" ([US 4032974](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4032974A/en)) [DS]. Flare correction integrated the average picture level and fed it into the clamp reference ([US 4001502](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4001502)) [DS]. The linear matrix came before gamma with rows summing to 1. BBC optimum matrices for Plumbicon cameras are close to `[1.14 −0.18 0.04; −0.06 1.23 −0.17; −0.03 0.02 1.01]`, and the matrix costs 0.3-0.7 dB of luminance S/N on white ([Jones 1968](https://worldradiohistory.com/Archive-Catalogs/Sellmeyer/Books/Color-Television-Journal-of-the-SMPTE-1970.pdf)) [DS]. Gamma was electronic, because the tube is linear. The choices of the era were the BBC 0.4 law (break 3.77 %, slope 5) and the 1.099·V^0.45 − 0.099 law (break 1.8 %, slope 4.5) ([EBU Tech 3335](https://tech.ebu.ch/files/live/sites/tech/files/shared/tech/tech3335.pdf)) [DS]. A 1984 patent puts the conventional knee at 100 % of rated light and white clip at 230 % ([US 4470067](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4470067A/en)) [DS].

Aperture correction is a set of delay-line operations on the line signal, which fits the simulator's rule of processing the continuous signal. The BBC corrector takes the 1-line-delayed signal minus half the sum of the undelayed and 2-line-delayed signals for vertical detail. For horizontal detail it uses a 150 ns unterminated line, "the input and reflected signals, which are 300 nanoseconds apart", followed by a "coring" circuit ([BBC Engineering 91](https://worldradiohistory.com/UK/BBC/BBC-Technical/BBC-Engineering/bbc-engineering-91-OCR.pdf)) [DS]. A vertical gain of `g ≤ 0.25` is the practical limit, and noise rises as `sqrt(1 + 2g²)` ([BBC Monograph 47](https://worldradiohistory.com/UK/BBC/BBC-Technical/BBC-Monograph/bbc-monograph-47-OCR.pdf)) [DS]. The horizontal 3-tap kernel `d(t) = s(t−T) − ½[s(t) + s(t−2T)]` has response `1 + k(1 − cos 2πfT)`, peaking at `1/(2T)` [DER]. Halos are symmetric, ±k/2 high and T wide. Restoring a 400 TVL response of 35-55 % to unity needs **+5 to +9 dB** at 5 MHz [DER]. Encoding then limited colour: NTSC I to 1.3 MHz and Q to 0.4-0.5 MHz, with delay matched to ±0.05 µs ([Loughren, NTSC](https://worldradiohistory.com/Archive-Catalogs/Sellmeyer/Books/Color-Television-Journal-of-the-SMPTE-1970.pdf)) [DS]. Studio head amplifiers and camera paths were flat to about 5-5.5 MHz ([US 3743766](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3743766A/en)) [DS].

## Single-tube camcorders trade colour bandwidth for a striped carrier

Home cameras of 1976-1990 put a colour-stripe filter on the faceplate of one 2/3-inch or 1/2-inch tube. Scanning the stripes turns colour into a spatial carrier whose frequency is the scan velocity divided by the stripe pitch. Panasonic gives `f = w·10³ / (p·t)` MHz: w = 7.49 mm effective width, p = 40 µm pitch and t = 52.3 µs active line give **3.58 MHz** ([Panasonic WV-3110](https://bh.hallikainen.org/uploads/harold/PanasonicWV3110v1.pdf)) [DS]. That is 143 m/s and about 187 carrier cycles per line [DER]. Three families existed. Frequency-separation systems with complementary cyan/yellow/clear stripes needed no phase reference and were "the most popular" consumer system ([Onishi et al. 1982](https://manuscript.isc.ac/Inventory/49/823974.htm)) [SEC]. Index systems with primary RGB stripes, such as Sony's Trinicon, add an index electrode buried in the target ([Wikipedia, Trinicon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinicon)) [SEC]. Two-tube luminance-plus-chroma designs were sold in 1976-78 ([Matsushita brochure](https://frank.pocnet.net/other/Matsushita/Matsushita_VidiconTubes.pdf)) [DS].

The Panasonic decoder shows the whole signal path. Cyan and yellow stripes of equal pitch and opposite slant shift the red and blue carriers 90° in opposite directions from line to line. A 3.58 MHz trap leaves luminance, "the G signal and the mean value of the modulated R and B signals", which feeds a **2.8 MHz** luminance low-pass and a **0.5 MHz** low-pass. A band-pass, a 90° shift and a 1H delay separate red (sum) from blue (difference); envelope detectors recover R and B, and these are re-encoded as NTSC ([Panasonic WV-3110](https://bh.hallikainen.org/uploads/harold/PanasonicWV3110v1.pdf)) [DS]. JVC's G/cyan/clear design uses 170 stripe groups for a 3.25 MHz fundamental and a 6.5 MHz second harmonic, with a 2.5 MHz luminance low-pass and ±500 kHz colour. Its signal model is `Sd = SG + (2/3)SB + (1/3)SR` ([US 4041528](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4041528)) [DS]. The Panasonic PK-958, a 2/3-inch Newvicon with a 5 MHz single carrier, specifies **300 TV lines, S/N above 45 dB and 7 lux minimum at f/1.4** ([PK-958](https://archive.org/stream/manual_PK958_SM_PANASONIC/PK958_SM_PANASONIC_djvu.txt)) [DS]. Sony's 1983 Betamovie BMC-100, with a 1/2-inch Saticon-target Trinicon, specifies 28 lux ([BMC-100](https://betamax.palsite.com/bmc100spec.html)) [SEC]. JVC's 1984 GR-C1 VHS-C camera is reported to use a 1/2-inch Saticon ([totalrewind](https://www.totalrewind.org/cameras/C_GRC1_main.htm)) [SEC].

The artefacts follow from the carrier, and the service manuals describe them [DS] ([Panasonic WV-3110](https://bh.hallikainen.org/uploads/harold/PanasonicWV3110v1.pdf)):

- **False colour.** Fine scene detail interferes with the stripes and produces false colour. A birefringent "crystal filter" acting as an optical low-pass is the cure, at a cost in sharpness.
- **Left-edge shading.** Coil hysteresis and the electrode fields at the start of the scan distort the scan and shade the left edge, so the deflection is deliberately non-linear.
- **Colour shading from focus.** Focus differs between centre and edge, so carrier modulation depth varies and "color shading appears". Dynamic focus corrects part of it.
- **Dark shading in luminance only.** Dark-current shading affects luminance but not the band-pass chroma, so colour shifts at low light and with temperature.
- **Green highlights.** When the beam cannot discharge the target, the R/B modulation riding on green saturates, and "the bright picture parts turn into an unnatural green color". A high-Y chroma clip limits this.
- **Weak modulation in dim areas.** Modulation depth in weakly lit areas "is reduced due to the over-beam", which forces separate R/B gamma tracking.
- **Green warm-up.** After switch-on the picture is green because the modulated components resolve last, and the iris stays closed for about 15 s.

The vidicon in that camera has gamma of about 0.65, so the correction gamma is only about 0.7 [DS]. Lag is high: Matsushita lists **20 %** at 50 ms for home-camera 2/3-inch vidicons, 25 % for the 1-inch striped S4077, **30 %** for the index RGB S4070, and 10-12 % for the Newvicons S4075/S4092 ([Matsushita brochure](https://frank.pocnet.net/other/Matsushita/Matsushita_VidiconTubes.pdf)) [DS]. The vidicon figures are at 10 lx and the Newvicon figures at 1 lx, so the comparison is not like-for-like. The stripe grid is fixed to the target, so residual charge is read through the same carrier. Lag should therefore show as coloured trails, a temporal low-pass on the R, B and Y envelopes, with no carrier smear [DER, unsourced]. The colour bandwidth of 0.5 MHz is about 40 TVL, about 10 % of picture width [DER].

## Burn-in is two processes: reversible trap memory and permanent damage

The sources separate burn-in into two kinds. One is a reversible trap memory, which can leave a negative image (lost sensitivity) or a positive one (excess dark current). The other is permanent damage. The only measured experiment is on Sb2S3. Two minutes of a 60 W lamp at 152 cm through f/1.5 cut sensitivity **from 0.080 to about 0.070 µA/µW (−12 %)** and raised dark current from 10 to nearly 11 nA. Both recovered within **30 minutes** ([US 3,546,520](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3546520A/en)) [DS]. The loss was largest at 600 nm (−20 %), and near-infrared light beyond about 0.7 µm caused it: a 7 mm KG-3 filter prevented it, and visible He-Ne light did not cause it [DS]. A 45 W·s photoflash, by contrast, left a spot with **about 100 nA dark current against 1 nA**, which did not recover [DS].

Lead oxide resists sticking but not completely. Specular-highlight burn in PbO is "more noticeable in new tubes" and occurs "primarily in the red and green channels". It "can be erased almost completely and almost instantly by exposing the camera to a white scene and cutting the beam current off for several seconds". It fades as the tube "develop[s] a more highly saturated characteristic" over several hundred hours ([RCA 1977, AN-5042](https://frank.pocnet.net/other/RCA/RCA_CameraReadyBoroadcastVidiconSeries_1977.pdf)) [DS]. Its cause is a target voltage too low to saturate the layer, which is worse for red because red light is absorbed throughout the layer [DS]; AN-5042 Fig. 2 gives the current-voltage curves (target voltage 10 / 13 / 15 / 20 / 25 / 30 / 35 / 40 / 45 / 60 / 95 V: blue light 175 / 195 / 210 / 230 / 238 / 245 / 246 / 248 / 249 / 252 / 256 nA; red light - / 150 / 172 / 205 / 225 / 233 / 240 / 248 / 251 / 258 / 270 nA) [FIG]. Permanent "blue slump" shows as a negative highlight burn and rising lag, first in blue and at the centre. A patent quantifies it by the ratio of blue sensitivity at 25 V to that at 50 V: good tubes are at or above 80 %, slumped tubes below. Regeneration lifted one tube from 65 % to 87 % ([US 4,090,758](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4090758)) [DS]. In the Saticon, the sign of the retained image depends on target voltage. RCA's rule is to lower the voltage for a positive burn and raise it for a negative one, within ±5 V of 50 V. The Toshiba Chalnicon rule for a positive after-image is the opposite: raise the voltage ([RCA 1977](https://frank.pocnet.net/other/RCA/RCA_CameraReadyBoroadcastVidiconSeries_1977.pdf); [Toshiba 1973](https://frank.yueksel.org/other/Toshiba/Toshiba_Chalnicon_1973.pdf)) [DS]. A burn term with a fixed sign would mispredict one of them [DER]. Hitachi measures Saticon "high-light sticking" as the ratio of the 10th field after cut-off from a 4 µA signal to a 0.2 µA reference ([US 4,330,733](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4330733)) [DS]. Damage thresholds are sparse. An RCA Saticon survives **21,500 lx for 20 s**. A Newvicon breaks down "instantaneously" if the sun is focused through an aperture wider than **f/11** ([RCA 1977](https://frank.pocnet.net/other/RCA/RCA_CameraReadyBoroadcastVidiconSeries_1977.pdf); [Philips T12](https://ftp.mirrorservice.org/sites/www.bitsavers.org/components/philips/_dataBooks/1987_T12_Philips_Vidicon_and_Newvicon_Camera_Tubes.pdf)) [DS]. Consumer cameras guarded against burn by holding the iris shut for 15 s at power-up and by cutting the tube voltages if deflection failed ([Panasonic WV-3110](https://bh.hallikainen.org/uploads/harold/PanasonicWV3110v1.pdf)) [DS]. Burn is also worse when the beam is off with light on the target ([US 4,492,982](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4492982A/en)) [DS]. Claims that Plumbicons resist burn better than Saticons, or that Saticons lag more, come only from an encyclopaedia without citations ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_camera_tube)) [SEC]. A primary source says the Saticon's persistence is "higher contrast than with a lead oxide tube but decays more rapidly" ([RCA 1977](https://frank.pocnet.net/other/RCA/RCA_CameraReadyBoroadcastVidiconSeries_1977.pdf)) [DS].

Temperature drives both dark current and lag. Dark current doubles every **7-8 °C** (Sb2S3, Newvicon) or **about 9 °C** (Saticon, Chalnicon) ([Philips T12](https://ftp.mirrorservice.org/sites/www.bitsavers.org/components/philips/_dataBooks/1987_T12_Philips_Vidicon_and_Newvicon_Camera_Tubes.pdf); [Hitachi H8397A](https://frank.yueksel.org/other/Hitachi/Hitachi_Saticon_H8397A_1977.pdf)) [DS]. The digitised curves read 0.15 nA at 20 °C to 2.7 nA at 60 °C for the H8397A, 0.033 nA at 0 °C to 2.0 nA at 55 °C for the BC4909, and 0.1 / 1 / 10 nA at 0 / 30 / 60 °C for the Chalnicon [FIG]. Expressed as an Arrhenius law, the doublings correspond to 0.6-0.8 eV [DER]. Because dark current acts as bias light, lag falls as the camera warms; Philips states this for the Newvicon [DS]. No dark-current curve against temperature exists for PbO, and no warm-up transient was found.

The per-channel K values already in the model absorb the PbO colour dependence (R, B and ER worse than L/G at equal beam conditions), which is legitimate. The physical reading, though, is a layer effect: trapping that depends on wavelength. A per-channel trap amplitude is the better parameter than a per-channel beam temperature [DER].

## A physically grounded camera model and the effects that dominate the look

### Model structure

The evidence supports a model with five state layers. Each is advanced in time as the beam scans, and the frame is written only after encoding and the recorder stage. Steps C and D replace a per-frame update with a per-line, per-field one. All the rest is either already present or new.

**A. Optics to photocurrent density.** Compute `j_c(x,y,t) = ∫ S_c(λ)·E(x,y,λ,t) dλ`, using each material's spectral knots, the channel filters and lens IR cut, and lens blur. Add target flare as a low-level halo (Saticon 1 %, vidicon 5 % on a 1/10-PH black patch). Add bias light j_b and dark current `j_d(T) = j_d0·2^((T−T0)/ΔT2)`. For single-tube presets, multiply by the stripe transmittance pattern behind an optical low-pass. The scene is sampled at the time each line integrates.

**B. Target state per element.** Each element has a surface potential V on a vertical grid finer than the line pitch (4-8 samples per frame line), with layer capacitance C' per unit area: about 1.1-2.2 nF per 30 mm target for PbO, 1000-2000 pF/cm² for the Saticon, larger for the Newvicon. Add one or two trap populations n_k that fill with photogeneration and release with time constants τ_k into the photocurrent. These are large for Sb2S3, small for PbO blue/ER, and about zero for the Saticon. Add a slow burn state (sensitivity factor and dark-current offset) whose sign depends on how far the target voltage is from saturation.

**C. Beam.** The scan path r(t) is a per-line ramp plus a low-order distortion field and per-tube registration offset. The spot is Gaussian with σ(x,y,Ib) per channel, about 0.65 of the frame-line pitch at centre and 1.5-1.7× that at the corners. Landing current density follows the acceptance curve: a saturated shoulder at the local beam density, an exponential region with VT = kT_b/e, and an optional hot fraction. ACT is a flyback clamp at the cathode-pulse level, spread over several lines. DBC raises beam current when signal current exceeds a threshold. Beam bending displaces landing toward elements whose potential exceeds their surroundings by several volts. Video current is the rate at which landed charge arrives, so signal ∝ scan speed and the horizontal aperture `σ_t = σ_x/v` come out without being added.

**D. Head amplifier and processing on the line signal.** Add current noise with the triangular spectrum in the linear current domain. Then apply head-amplifier bandwidth and optical-black clamp; flare (APL) subtraction; shading correction; linear matrix; knee; gamma; and aperture correction from delayed copies of the continuous signal (horizontal T = 0.15-0.2 µs, vertical 1H/2H) with coring; then clip.

**E. Encoding.** For three-tube presets, apply Y/chroma encoding with the chroma low-pass. For single-tube presets, run the stripe decoder: trap, band-pass, 90°/1H separation, envelope or synchronous detection, Y low-pass at 2.5-2.8 MHz and 0.5 MHz colour. The result goes to the VHS stage, and the frame is written last.

The logistic law is then not part of the code but a test of it. The full scan simulation, driven by the handbook test procedure (uniform field, 5 s on or 10 s off, stated bias, meter-averaged current), should reproduce the closed form and the curves.

## Open gaps

Several gaps limit how far a simulator can be validated against data:

- **Calibration data.** No measured comet-tail length or bloom radius against overload and beam current exists in the sources. Tail and spill strengths therefore stay calibrated against descriptions: "a number of scannings", "about 2 s", 32 × and 16 × capacity.
- **Trap models.** No trap time constants, densities or dispersive-transport fits were found for any target. The two-population trap term rests only on the exposure-duration curves.
- **Frequency and temperature coverage.** No 60 Hz Plumbicon lag curve, no PbO dark current against temperature, and no warm-up transient were found.
- **Integration window.** The hybrid field/frame integration caused by beam overlap is inferred, with no measurement found; Hasker 1971 and Philips Technical Review 1968 were not available.
- **Spot data.** Spot size in µm is published only for one 1-inch vidicon, without a stated diameter convention.
- **Consumer cameras.** Their data are thin: the only quantitative circuit-level documents are two Panasonic service manuals and a few patents. Trinicon stripe pitch, index frequency and target data, and the S/N and resolution of Sony, JVC and RCA consumer models, are missing. Nothing documents how lag interacts with the stripe carrier.
- **Burn-in kinetics.** These are measured only once, for Sb2S3 at a single exposure.
- **Electronics.** Era-specific coring thresholds, knee settings, aperture-boost values in dB and auto-iris loop constants are undocumented. The 1965-1990 knee and clip figures come from one 1984 patent.
- **Unexplained data.** The apparent Saticon lag rise with signal appears only in H8397A Fig. 6 and is contradicted by RCA's BC4908 (Neuhauser 1978 Fig. 12), Hitachi Hyoron 1976 and US 4,330,733; see [`docs/design/CAMERA_RETENTION.md`](design/CAMERA_RETENTION.md), "The Saticon's lag does not rise with signal".

## Conclusion

The physics is more economical than the existing model's parameter list suggests. A single readout equation, `C·dV/dt = j_photo + j_dark + j_bias − I0·exp(V/VT)` stepped line by line under a Gaussian beam with a saturating shoulder, accounts for several effects at once: low-key lag, build-up and decay asymmetry, the effect of bias light, high-key lag, time-based persistence, comet tails, and part of the interlace behaviour. It does so with a physical beam temperature and a layer capacitance in the published ranges. The fitted VT of 0.05-0.07 V and the "missing" 2-3.5 % bias tail come from the parameterisation and from a misleading table column. What the equation does not cover is narrow and identifiable: wavelength-dependent traps that fill over about a second (PbO blue, extended red, and above all Sb2S3), beam bending, and burn states whose sign depends on target voltage. These should be the only additions beyond the scan itself.

For tube video on VHS specifically, the evidence shifts the emphasis away from studio-camera detail. The dominant camera signatures are temporal and tonal, and they survive a narrow-band recorder: lag trails of 10-30 %, comet tails and green or red highlight artefacts, shadow texture lifted by gamma, and for camcorders the 0.5 MHz stripe-decoded colour. Fine-detail effects such as the 5 MHz spot response, 30 ns registration and the head amplifier's f² noise mostly fall above the recording band. They matter for three-tube studio sources, but they are unlikely to decide whether a consumer recording looks right.
