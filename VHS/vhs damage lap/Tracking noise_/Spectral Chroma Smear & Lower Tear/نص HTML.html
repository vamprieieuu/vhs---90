VHSart — IMPLEMENT SPECTRAL CHROMA SMEAR & LOWER TRACKING TEAR

Objective

Reconstruct the supplied visual reference IMG_4654.jpeg as a dedicated, fully procedural effect inside the native C++ After Effects plugin, VHSart.

The reference has a distinctive two-part visual structure:

  1. Upper and Mid-Frame Spectral Chroma Smear: Extremely saturated, irregular rainbow-like chromatic bleeding and spectral color displacement spreading across the upper and middle portions of the image.
  2. Lower Horizontal Tracking Interference: A dense, full-width horizontal tracking and signal-interference band occupying approximately the bottom 35–40% of the frame, containing horizontal tearing, scanline fragmentation, RGB displacement, colored noise, and bright/dark dropouts.

These two components must be independently generated and controlled, while visually interacting where their regions meet.

The effect must be reconstructed mathematically and procedurally. Do not use pre-rendered overlays, stock textures, images, video clips, GIFs, or baked-in reference material.

Before implementation:

  • Inspect the complete VHSart project and rendering architecture.
  • Identify existing chroma, RGB displacement, color processing, tracking, tearing, dropout, grain, scanline, softness, and noise engines.
  • Reuse or extend existing compatible engines.
  • Do not duplicate functionality that already exists.
  • Build a dedicated engine only for the distinctive spectral chroma distribution and lower-band tracking behavior that existing engines cannot reproduce accurately.
  • Preserve all existing effects, presets, parameters, and functionality.

Do not assume existing functionality works correctly without inspecting its actual implementation.

2. Dedicated Engine Architecture

Implement a dedicated hybrid engine:

Spectral Chroma & Lower Tear Engine

It must contain two independently configurable processing systems:

A. Spectral Chroma Smear System

Responsible for the upper and middle frame’s saturated spectral color bleeding.

B. Lower Tracking Tear System

Responsible for the dense horizontal tracking-interference region occupying the lower portion of the frame.

Both systems must have independent controls, masks, intensity, and seed behavior.

They must not be implemented as one generic glitch operation with different colors.

3. Spectral Chroma Smear & Shift

Reconstruct the broad, irregular, highly saturated rainbow-like chromatic contamination visible across the upper and middle portions of the reference.

The color behavior must include:

  • Red.
  • Orange.
  • Yellow.
  • Green.
  • Cyan.
  • Blue.
  • Violet.
  • Magenta.

Generate irregular, overlapping spectral color regions rather than a simple linear rainbow gradient.

The chromatic appearance should resemble broad analog color bleeding and spectral contamination:

  • Organic, uneven color boundaries.
  • Wide chromatic transitions.
  • Localized color displacement.
  • Horizontal chroma extensions.
  • Some vertical and diagonal color spreading.
  • Overlapping color regions.
  • High saturation with controlled brightness interaction.
  • Irregular spectral distribution across the image.

The spectral color behavior must interact with the underlying footage rather than simply cover it with opaque colored shapes.

Preserve recognizable scene structure where the reference retains it.

Spatial Distribution

The spectral effect should primarily occupy the upper and middle portions of the frame.

Use an adjustable spatial mask with:

  • Upper-region influence.
  • Middle-region influence.
  • Adjustable vertical falloff.
  • Adjustable horizontal distribution.
  • Irregular organic variation.
  • Optional localized spectral regions.

The mask must not be permanently restricted to a fixed 50% upper-frame rectangle. Its distribution should be adjustable.

Chroma Processing

Implement appropriate chroma-space or RGB-space processing, including:

  • Horizontal chroma smearing.
  • Localized channel displacement.
  • Spectral hue spreading.
  • Saturation enhancement.
  • Controlled chroma bleeding.
  • Optional subtle luminance interaction.

Prefer chroma-aware processing where possible so that luminance detail does not become unnecessarily destroyed.

Do not turn the entire image into a uniform rainbow gradient.

4. Lower Horizontal Tracking & Interference Band

Create a dedicated lower-frame tracking-interference region.

Default starting position:

  • Lower Band Start: approximately 60% of frame height.
  • Coverage: approximately 35–40% of frame height.
  • Horizontal coverage: full frame width.

The band must contain:

  • Irregular horizontal scanline displacement.
  • Broken and fragmented horizontal strips.
  • Uneven horizontal tearing.
  • Bright white and gray streaks.
  • Dark interruptions.
  • Red, green, blue, cyan, violet, and magenta interference fragments.
  • Localized RGB separation.
  • Chroma displacement.
  • Dense signal noise.
  • Irregular dropout segments.
  • Abrupt local brightness changes.

The result must look like a damaged analog tracking region, not a conventional clean rectangular glitch bar.

Horizontal Displacement

Apply row-based horizontal displacement inside the lower-band mask:

sourceX = x - displacement(y, x, time, seed)

Use a combination of:

  • Smooth low-frequency row offsets.
  • Abrupt displacement jumps.
  • Fine horizontal jitter.
  • Segmented scanline shifts.
  • Irregular displacement amplitude.

The displacement must remain predominantly horizontal.

Internal Fragmentation

Generate independently varying horizontal segments with:

  • Different lengths.
  • Different brightness.
  • Different color contamination.
  • Different displacement amplitudes.
  • Irregular gaps.
  • Nonuniform density.

Avoid identical repeating scanlines or synchronized flashing across the entire band.

5. Interaction Between the Two Main Systems

The upper/middle spectral chroma system and the lower tracking system must remain independently adjustable.

However, their boundary should support natural interaction.

For example:

  • Spectral color bleeding may extend slightly into the upper edge of the tracking band.
  • The lower band may introduce additional chromatic fragmentation.
  • RGB displacement may become stronger inside the tracking region.
  • The spectral system must not automatically become disabled when the lower band is enabled.

Provide separate controls for each system’s intensity and spatial coverage.

6. Global Analog Base

Reuse VHSart’s existing engines for:

  • Low-resolution analog character.
  • Mild softness.
  • Fine global grain.
  • Subtle scanlines.
  • High-frequency detail reduction.

The base effect must remain secondary to the two main components.

Provide independent softness, grain, and scanline controls where they are not already available through existing shared controls.

Avoid redundant parameters.

7. Processing Pipeline

Use the following logical processing order:

  1. Existing low-resolution / softness processing.
  2. Existing global grain and fine scanline processing.
  3. Spectral chroma mask generation.
  4. Spectral chroma bleeding and hue displacement.
  5. Lower tracking-band mask generation.
  6. Lower horizontal displacement and tearing.
  7. Localized RGB/chroma fragmentation.
  8. Dropouts, brightness flicker, and interference noise.
  9. Final compositing and alpha handling.

Adjust the exact implementation order if the existing rendering architecture requires it, but preserve the independent behavior and spatial masks of both systems.
