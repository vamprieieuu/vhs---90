VHSart — ADVANCED TRACKING NOISE & SNOW ENGINE

Multi-Plugin Research, Source-Based Reconstruction & Advanced Preset System

1. Main Objective

Expand the VHSart Tracking Noise system into a comprehensive, highly configurable analog signal degradation engine.

The goal is to research, reconstruct, and combine the distinctive Tracking Noise and Snow characteristics found in:

1. Signal Plugin
2. NTSC R
3. VHS Tape
4. Universal VHS
5. VHS Pro

Do not assume these plugins use identical algorithms. Investigate each implementation independently, identify its actual behavior, and preserve the distinctive characteristics that make each one visually recognizable.

The final result must be a unified and extensible engine with multiple genuinely different presets, not five superficial copies of the original plugins.

2. Source Code and Reverse-Engineering Research

Use the actual available materials as the primary research sources:

* NTSC R: Inspect the available original source code. Study its Tracking Noise implementation, parameters, internal calculations, rendering behavior, and related Snow/Noise controls.
* VHS Tape: Inspect the available AEX binary and the engineer’s Ghidra reverse-engineering materials, including relevant C and H files. Recover as much implementation detail and parameter behavior as technically possible.
* Signal Plugin: Investigate its actual Tracking Noise and Tape-related behavior using available source materials and technical references.
* Universal VHS and VHS Pro: Examine all available source code, binaries, extracted files, interfaces, and documented behavior.

If usable source code exists and its license and compatibility permit reuse, evaluate whether it can be integrated directly.

Otherwise, reconstruct the behavior independently using the available technical evidence.

Do not invent implementation details or claim exact reconstruction when the necessary source or evidence is unavailable.

3. Tracking Noise — Expanded Position and Coverage System

The Tracking Noise engine must support considerably more than a conventional horizontal tracking band.

A. Tracking Noise Height

Implement a genuinely extended Height control.

The Height must be capable of increasing continuously until the Tracking Noise covers the entire image.

For example:

* Minimum: no visible tracking band.
* Low: thin tracking band.
* Medium: partial image coverage.
* High: large tracking region.
* Maximum: complete image coverage.

Do not inherit arbitrary parameter limits from NTSC R. If its original Height parameter stops at 120, investigate what that value means internally, but design VHSart’s control to support full-frame coverage.

At maximum coverage, the original image should be capable of becoming almost or completely obscured by the Tracking Noise.

The result must remain visually convincing rather than simply stretching a static noise texture.

B. Tracking Noise Position and Direction

Allow the user to position Tracking Noise around the image, not just in one fixed horizontal region.

Support:

* Top
* Bottom
* Left
* Right
* Multiple simultaneous regions
* Freely adjustable positioning where technically practical

Each region must have independent controls for:

* Position
* Height or Width
* Coverage
* Intensity
* Displacement
* Edge behavior
* Noise density

For example, users must be able to create a tracking band entering from the top, another emerging from the bottom, or vertical tracking interference along either side.

C. Tracking Noise Expansion

Implement controls that allow the user to extend and enlarge the tracking region.

The user must be able to increase its coverage progressively until it occupies the entire frame.

Support independent control over horizontal and vertical expansion, with appropriate behavior for horizontal and vertical tracking regions.

D. Wave Intensity

Implement a dedicated Wave Intensity control.

Research how the source plugins generate their tracking waves and reproduce the underlying behavior where possible.

The control should affect the intensity and deformation of the tracking interference, not merely increase ordinary grain.

E. Tracking Noise Enable/Disable

Provide an independent enable/disable switch for Tracking Noise.

Disabling it must not reset or destroy its saved parameter values.

4. Advanced Snow Engine

Snow must be treated as a complete signal-degradation system, not as a single generic noise slider.

Research the Snow behavior in NTSC R, VHS Pro, VHS Tape, Universal VHS, and Signal Plugin.

Combine the useful characteristics into a detailed Snow engine while preserving meaningful differences between the source implementations.

Required controls:

* Snow Enable/Disable
* Snow Intensity
* Snow Density
* Snow Size
* Snow Distribution
* Snow Contrast
* Snow Brightness
* Snow Color
* Monochrome/Colored Snow
* Temporal Variation
* Randomness
* Animation Speed
* Seed

Investigate the actual Snow implementation in NTSC R before deciding what each original parameter does.

In particular, inspect the parameter referred to in the source/interface as Snow Inspiratory (verify its exact name and purpose rather than guessing). Determine whether it controls a particular snow-generation behavior, modulation, distribution, or another signal characteristic.

Do not discard unusual source-specific controls just because their meaning is initially unclear.

If the original behavior can be recovered, implement it accurately. If it cannot, document the uncertainty and develop an independently designed equivalent only when technically justified.

5. Additional Noise Intensity

Implement a separate Noise Intensity control for the noise occurring beneath or around the Tracking Noise region.

Do not automatically combine this with Snow Intensity.

Investigate whether the source plugins distinguish between:

* Tracking-band noise
* Snow/static
* Background signal noise
* General image noise
* Chroma noise
* Luma interference

Preserve these distinctions wherever the source behavior supports them.

6. Source-Inspired Preset Collection

Create a collection of independent presets inspired by the actual characteristics discovered in the five plugins.

Each preset must have a new VHSart name.

Do not use original plugin names in preset names.

Do not create five presets that differ only in color or intensity.

Presets must preserve distinctive differences in:

* Tracking-band geometry
* Position and movement
* Height and coverage
* Wave behavior
* Snow generation
* Noise distribution
* Signal displacement
* Chroma and luma degradation
* Temporal behavior

The user should be able to select a preset inspired by a particular source and still edit all relevant controls independently.

The reference image previously supplied for the four-edge Tracking Noise analysis must also be considered as an independent visual target.

7. Unified Engine Architecture

Before implementing anything, inspect the complete VHSart project and identify existing engines, especially:

* Localized Tear & Chroma Burst
* Existing Tracking Noise functionality
* Existing Static/Snow generators
* Existing signal displacement and chroma systems

Extend or reuse existing engines wherever technically appropriate.

Do not create duplicate engines for functionality that already exists.

The final architecture should support multiple independently configurable Tracking Noise regions, source-inspired behaviors, and advanced Snow generation.

8. Visual and Technical Requirements

All effects must be generated procedurally during rendering.

Do not use pre-rendered images, videos, GIFs, or static noise overlays as the actual effect implementation.

Preserve:

* Native C++ After Effects SDK compatibility
* Alpha and premultiplied-alpha correctness
* Multi-Frame Rendering compatibility
* Thread safety
* Deterministic rendering when using a fixed seed
* Independent parameter control
* Existing VHSart functionality and presets

9. Testing and Validation

Test the engine at multiple Tracking Noise heights, including complete frame coverage.

Test all four edge positions independently and in combination.

Test extreme Snow Intensity values and combinations of Snow, Wave Intensity, Tracking Noise, and background Noise Intensity.

Verify that:

* Tracking Noise can completely obscure the original image.
* Top, bottom, left, and right positioning work correctly.
* Multiple regions can coexist.
* Height and coverage controls behave progressively.
* Snow remains independently adjustable.
* Source-inspired presets produce genuinely distinct results.
* Existing VHSart engines are not broken.

10. Final Expected Result

Deliver a distinctive VHSart Tracking Noise and Snow system built from the combined technical research of all five source plugins.

The final system must offer:

1. Source-inspired Tracking Noise behaviors.
2. Full-frame Tracking Noise coverage.
3. Four-edge and multi-region positioning.
4. Independent region sizing and intensity.
5. Wave Intensity.
6. A comprehensive Snow Engine.
7. Separate background Noise Intensity.
8. A collection of visually distinct, editable presets.

Important: The objective is not to imitate the appearance of a few reference screenshots. The objective is to understand the original implementations wherever possible, reconstruct their actual signal behavior, and combine their strongest characteristics into a much more capable VHSart engine.
